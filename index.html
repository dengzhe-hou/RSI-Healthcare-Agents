Abstract

Advances in persistent memory, reusable skills and adaptive training are making recursive self-improvement (RSI) increasingly relevant to healthcare agents.

RSI concerns changes to the procedures that generate future improvements. This survey connects these developments with healthcare evidence by distinguishing component updates, training-generator changes and revisions of improvement procedures. We examine applications in diagnosis, reporting and question answering, together with challenges in evaluation, reliability, privacy and update governance. Current evidence supports persistent component updates and selected generator gains, while the effects of revised improvement procedures on subsequent learning remain untested in the mapped studies. An accompanying evidence map and evaluation templates support study comparison and future research.
